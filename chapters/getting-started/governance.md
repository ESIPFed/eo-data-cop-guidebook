# CoP Charter

## Purpose & Value Proposition
### Purpose 
The ICESat-2 Data-Centric CoP exists to unite scientists, data engineers, and researchers working with ICESat-2 data. By fostering an open, collaborative environment, we aim to standardize data pipelines, break down departmental and institutional data silos, and champion open science practices.
Value Proposition: We reduce duplicated efforts and accelerate "time-to-science" by sharing best practices, tools, and code. This CoP provides a centralized forum to solve common technical challenges associated with processing, analyzing, and managing large-scale ICESat-2 datasets, ensuring teams can leverage the full potential of the mission's data.
Scope

## Scope
### Data Use & Management
Scope focus: Maximizing the value, accessibility, and understanding of data.
* Data Discovery & Access: Sharing methods for querying, downloading, or accessing data (e.g., via APIs, cloud storage buckets, or institutional archives).
* Data Processing & Quality: Discussing best practices for cleaning, calibrating, formatting, and handling missing or anomalous data.
* Data Fusion: Exploring techniques for integrating disparate datasets (e.g., combining satellite observations with ground-sensor data or socioeconomic models).
* Data Governance & Ethics: Navigating data security levels, proper attribution/citations, and adopting FAIR (Findable, Accessible, Interoperable, Reusable) data principles.

### Sharing Research & Application Ideas
* Scope focus: Fostering scientific cross-pollination and accelerating the transition from pure research to practical application.
* Use-Case Showcases Presenting ongoing or completed projects to highlight how data is being used to solve specific scientific or societal problems.
* Ideation & Brainstorming: Providing a "safe space" to pitch early-stage research questions, seek feedback on experimental methodologies, or brainstorm new applications for existing datasets.
* Methodology Discussions: Debating the pros and cons of different analytical approaches (e.g., traditional statistical models vs. machine learning algorithms for specific datasets).
* Research-to-Operations (R2O): Sharing strategies for packaging research models so they can be adopted by operational agencies, policymakers, or end-users.

### Sharing Workflows & Tooling
Scope focus: Reducing duplicated effort and standardizing technical approaches.
Code & Pipeline Sharing: Exchanging scripts (Python, R, Julia, etc.), Jupyter Notebooks, and data processing pipelines to prevent "reinventing the wheel."
Reproducibility: Establishing standards for containerization (e.g., Docker), version control (e.g., GitHub/GitLab), and environment management to ensure others can run the shared workflows.
Infrastructure & Cloud: Sharing tips for optimizing workflows on high-performance computing (HPC) clusters or cloud environments (e.g., managing compute costs, parallel processing, and memory optimization).
Tool Evaluation: Collectively reviewing and recommending open-source libraries, software packages, and visualization tools.

### Building Collaborations
Scope focus: Connecting people across silos to build high-functioning, interdisciplinary teams.
* Matchmaking: Connecting individuals with domain expertise (e.g., a hydrologist) with individuals possessing technical expertise (e.g., a machine learning engineer) to tackle joint problems.
* Proposal Teaming: Identifying opportunities for joint funding proposals, white papers, or co-authored publications.
* Mentorship & Skill Transfer: Creating pathways for senior researchers/engineers to mentor junior staff or students in specific technical or scientific domains.
* Breaking Silos: Actively inviting participation from different departments, partner institutions, and external stakeholders to broaden the community's perspective.

## Roles & Responsibilities
To ensure sustainability without creating bureaucratic bottlenecks, the CoP operates with a lightweight governance model:
* Facilitators / Co-Leads: Guide the strategic vision of the CoP. They ensure discussions remain aligned with the charter, foster engagement, and advocate for the community’s needs to broader stakeholders.
* Meeting Coordinators: Handle the logistics of the CoP. Responsibilities include scheduling meetings, managing calendar invites, organizing guest speakers, and ensuring notes or recordings are distributed.
* Working Group Leads: Subject matter experts who volunteer to drive specific, time-bound initiatives (e.g., developing a specific machine learning tutorial, or drafting a white paper on cloud optimization).
* General Participants: The core of the community. Participants are expected to actively engage, bring their challenges to the group, share their localized solutions, and provide constructive feedback.

## Cadence & Channels
* Communication Platform: Microsoft Teams (or Slack) for daily asynchronous chat, quick technical Q&A, and sharing announcements. 
* Repository Location: GitHub (internal or public, depending on clearance) for shared codebases, Jupyter notebooks, and open-source tooling. An internal Wiki/Confluence space will be used for onboarding documents, meeting minutes, and architectural diagrams.
* Meeting Frequency:
  * Monthly Webinars / Show-and-Tells: A 1-hour session featuring presentations on new tools, successful project use-cases, or deep-dives into specific ICESat-2 data products.* 
  * Bi-Weekly Co-Working / Office Hours: A 45-minute informal, drop-in session for live troubleshooting, pair programming, and answering technical questions.

## Community Norms & Participation
* Psychological Safety: We foster a blameless, welcoming environment. Technical questions of all levels—from "How do I read an HDF5 file?" to complex machine learning architecture queries—are encouraged and respected. 
* Open-Knowledge Sharing: "Default to open" (within NASA data security guidelines). We share our successes as well as our failures and bottlenecks so the community can learn collectively.
* Contribution Guidelines: Code and documentation contributions should be peer-reviewed via Pull Requests to ensure quality. Feedback should always be constructive, respectful, and actionable.
## Success Metrics
Early Wins (0-3 Months):
* Establishment and population of the CoP communication channel (e.g., Teams) and GitHub repository.
* Execution of the first three successful CoP meetings with consistent attendance.
* Creation and publication of a "Getting Started with ICESat-2 Data" shared tutorial/notebook.
## Measures of Impact (6-12 Months):
* Cross-Team Integration: Documented instances of different departments or teams utilizing a shared data pipeline or tool.
* Repository Utilization: Number of active contributors, Pull Requests, and internal forks of shared CoP repositories.
* Engagement: Sustained or growing monthly active participation in meetings and chat channels.
* Efficiency: Qualitative feedback indicating a reduction in time spent on data wrangling and pipeline setup for new project members.
